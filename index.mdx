Soran is a naming protocol built on Stellar. Organizations operate **namespaces**, people hold **names** inside them, and apps use a shared registry to find what those names point to.

<Illustration name="ecosystem" priority alt="A shared registry connects nova, orbit and terra to their namespace contracts and names such as alice.nova." caption="Illustrative namespaces: different organizations, one shared naming system." />

A name like `alice.nova` can carry Alice's profile, receiving addresses on several blockchains, and names such as `shop.alice.nova` beneath it. One name gives apps a place to look for the information Alice chooses to publish.

## A namespace of your own

<span id="claim-once-then-issue-names" />

In `alice.nova`, **nova** is the namespace. **alice** is a name within it. A wallet, community, or business can operate a namespace and offer names to its users under its own registration rules.

Think of the coordination role ICANN plays in the Internet's naming system: different organizations operate different parts of a shared system. Soran brings that organizational pattern to on-chain namespaces. These are Stellar-based names with Soran's own rules; they do not automatically become Internet DNS domains.

An organization [applies for a namespace](/concepts/claiming-a-namespace). Public applications pass through an objection window before they can be awarded. The **Registry** records who owns each namespace and which contracts manage it.

The useful part is what happens between organizations: an app that integrates Soran can look up a name from another namespace through the same shared infrastructure.

## The Registrar: how people get names

Each namespace has a **Registrar**, a smart contract that applies its rules for issuing and managing names. The namespace owner chooses who can register, whether a name has a price, and the supported ownership policy.

<Illustration name="registrar" alt="The nova namespace owner sets registration rules; its Registrar applies them when issuing alice.nova to Alice." />

You might offer free names during signup, let eligible users claim their own names, or issue them from your app. The person or account holding a name is its **holder**. That holder manages the name's records under the namespace's policy.

Explore [automatic username claiming](/owner/automatic-claiming) or [names and namespaces](/concepts/names-and-namespaces).

## A name with a profile

A Soran name can carry an avatar, description, website, and social links. Apps can read those records to show a recognizable identity alongside an address. The holder publishes the profile; social links are self-described rather than independently verified accounts.

<Illustration name="profiles" alt="An alice.nova profile card with a portrait, website and social links connects to supporting wallet and community apps." caption="A holder-published profile gives apps a name, avatar, and links to display." />

A receiving account can also choose a **display name** within a namespace and a **primary name** across namespaces. Apps verify that the chosen name still points back to the account before using it.

Learn about [profiles and social identity](/concepts/profiles), then [publish your profile](/holder/profile).

## One name, addresses across blockchains

The **Resolver** stores the records a name points to. For Stellar, that includes an address and any required payment memo. It can also store a separate receiving address for each additional network the namespace enables.

<Illustration name="multichain" alt="alice.nova resolves to a separate destination for Stellar, Ethereum, Bitcoin or Solana, according to the network the app requests." caption="The sender chooses a network. The name supplies the destination for that network." />

Soran supports address records for Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, and other [supported networks](/concepts/multichain-addresses#supported-networks). A wallet reads the requested network's record, then handles the payment on that network. Name resolution does not move funds.

Stellar destinations keep their routing details together: a classic account with an optional memo, a complete muxed address, or a contract address. Read [Stellar payment destinations](/concepts/payment-destinations) or [add network addresses](/holder/network-addresses).

## Names beneath your name

When your namespace enables subnames, you can create one additional level beneath a name you hold. Alice might use `pay.alice.nova` for personal payments and `shop.alice.nova` for her store.

<Illustration name="subnames" alt="Alice controls alice.nova and its two child names, pay.alice.nova and shop.alice.nova, each with separate receiving details." />

Each child can have its own addresses and profile records. The parent holder controls it, even when its receiving address belongs to someone else. Children follow their parent's ownership and lifetime; transferring the parent invalidates its old children.

Read [how subnames work](/concepts/subnames) or [create a subname](/holder/subnames). Additional network addresses and subnames require compatible namespace contracts and the owner's [feature settings](/owner/feature-settings).

## How apps read the shared system

<span id="one-on-chain-read-entry-point" />

**Universal Lookup** is the common read entry point. It follows the Registry to the namespace's contracts, checks the name, and returns the requested current records. A wallet can read names without running a separate address book for every organization.

<Illustration name="resolution" alt="An app looks up alice.nova through Soran Lookup to read a Stellar address and memo, profile, or enabled network address." caption="Lookup reads the current records. The wallet handles any payment separately." />

The SDKs make these reads and name-management actions easier to integrate. You can also use the HTTP API or call the contracts directly. [Explore the protocol architecture](/concepts/architecture).

<span id="read-the-ownership-policy" />

Each namespace's policies and contracts determine which changes remain possible. A name without an expiry is not automatically permanent. [Understand ownership and governance](/concepts/ownership-guarantees) before choosing a namespace.
