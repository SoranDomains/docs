A Soran name can tell an app who you are as well as where to send a payment. You can attach an avatar, a short description, a website and social handles to `alice.nova`. A supporting app reads those records from Soran instead of asking you to create the same profile again.

This is Soran's social identity layer: a name you control, public information you choose to publish, and a way for apps to recognize your wallet.

## What belongs in a profile?

Soran defines eight standard fields that integrations can look for:

| Field | Example use |
| --- | --- |
| `avatar` | The URL of your profile image |
| `description` | A short introduction |
| `url` | Your personal or organization website |
| `org` | Your organization |
| `location` | A location you choose to share |
| `email` | A public contact address |
| `twitter` | Your Twitter/X handle |
| `github` | Your GitHub handle |

You choose which fields to publish. A designer might share a portfolio and avatar; a community contributor might share a GitHub handle and short biography. Applications decide which fields to display. They can also agree on additional text-record keys for their own integrations.

The Resolver verifies that the current holder authorized a record. It does not verify that a social account, organization or website belongs to that person. Treat profile information as the holder's public statement.

## Connect a wallet to a name

Profiles and display names work together. A **namespace display name** lets an app look up an address and find its chosen name within a namespace. A **primary name** selects one of those verified names across namespaces.

For example, Alice can publish a profile on `alice.nova`, then choose it as her wallet's primary name. A supporting wallet can show that name and profile beside her address.

The receiving address authorizes its display-name election, and the name must resolve back to that address. Holding a name that pays someone else does not grant control over the recipient's identity.

## Your records follow your control of the name

The holder publishes profile records through the Holder SDK or a configured MCP integration. The current Soran app provides display-name and primary-name controls; profile-field publishing uses those developer tools. For subname profiles, use the Holder SDK: published MCP 0.9.6 accepts ordinary `name.namespace` names only.

If ownership changes, the former holder's records stop appearing as the new holder's profile. An expired name does not provide an active identity. For a [subname](/concepts/subnames), the parent holder controls its profile.

Profile records are public on-chain text. Clearing a field retracts its current value by writing an empty string; it does not erase blockchain history. Publish information you intend to share publicly.
