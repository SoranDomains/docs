A public namespace claim follows **announce → objection window → execute** on chain. The fee is for a namespace such as `nova`, not individual names such as `alice.nova`.

<Note>
The fresh testnet Allocator is deployed with a 5,000 native XLM fee and a verified receiving/governance address. The proposed mainnet price is 5,000 USDC, not a configured live price. See [release status](/reference/release-status).
</Note>

## Pay upfront into escrow

The Allocator must have a governance-configured fee policy before any public `announce` can succeed. That policy names the token, positive base-unit amount and recipient. Configuration is one-time in this implementation; a claim never relies on an off-chain price alone.

The claimant signs an announcement that transfers the exact fee into the Allocator. The payment stays reserved while the claim is pending. Objection bonds use a separate accounting system and may use a different token.

The Allocator's governance address cannot itself announce a public claim. Use the actual claimant's authorized account; being the configured fee recipient does not grant an exemption.

The app and local MCP claim flow require a reviewed fee quote and an explicit network-fee ceiling. They re-read the on-chain fee policy and check the exact authorization before signing. The namespace fee and Stellar transaction/resource fees are separate.

## The claim window

The deployed Allocator sets its window and maximum claim age. The fresh testnet configuration uses a one-day objection window; check actual deployed configuration before relying on a countdown. Removing Universal Lookup's upgrade delay does **not** remove the claim window.

During the open window an eligible third party may object with a bond and evidence. An objection pauses the claim for governance adjudication. Once unopposed and ready, `execute` is permissionless and awards the namespace through Registry. Automation can submit it, but a countdown does not prove execution occurred.

An allocated namespace belongs to its recorded owner. The Allocator cannot reassign an already allocated Registry node; the owner can still transfer its namespace through the Registry's transfer process.

## Fee outcomes

| Outcome | Claimant entitlement | Configured recipient entitlement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awarded | 0% | 100% |
| Rejected after an upheld objection | 100% | 0% |
| Provably stuck claim recovery | 100% | 0% |
| Withdrawn | 80% | 20% |
| Expired and cleared | 80% | 20% |

For 80% refunds, integer base units are rounded down and the remainder goes to the recipient. Contract-recognized stuck recovery takes precedence when the claim is also expired or withdrawn.

**Stuck has a specific meaning:** the claim is still announced or objected while Registry already has an owner for that namespace, so the claim cannot award it. `cancel_stuck` checks that condition on chain. For an objected stuck claim, the outstanding objection bond is also returned in full. A slow network, an active objection, a missed deadline or general dissatisfaction does not itself qualify for a 100% stuck refund.

Settlement attempts to pay the entitled parties. If delivery fails, the undelivered amount becomes a protected **pull credit**. Anyone can trigger its delivery, but it always goes to the recorded payee. A settled receipt reports entitlements, not proof that every transfer already arrived. Fee reserves and credits cannot be swept as surplus.

## Objection outcomes

- An **upheld** objection rejects the claim, returns its fee in full and refunds the objector's bond.
- A **dismissed** objection resumes the existing claim; it does not charge the claim fee again. The objection bond is burned under the Allocator's rules.
- An adjudication timeout provides a permissionless recovery path and resumes the claim under the timeout rules; it is not a fee rejection.
- Rejected-claim cooldowns, claimant reopen procedures and bounded objection cycles remain separate contract rules. Rejection does not permanently reserve the label for an objector.

Withdrawal is claimant-authorized for eligible announced or objected claims. Expiry clearing and stuck recovery are permissionless when their on-chain conditions hold. An objected withdrawal also settles the objection bond under the withdrawal rules; the namespace fee refund remains 80% unless the applicable stuck condition is established.

## Reserved labels

Registry commits reserved labels into an immutable Merkle root. Reservation is not ownership.

- A **bound reserved claimant** can claim directly with the required proof while that reservation is active. This direct Registry path bypasses Allocator escrow and is exempt from the namespace fee.
- Unbound reservations and public claims after an allowed fallback deadline use the public Allocator process and pay its fee.
- An arbitrary user cannot bypass the fee by calling Registry's public allocation path: that path is restricted to the Allocator, apart from valid reserved direct claims.

`isAvailable(namespace)` checks ownership. It does not prove a label is unrestricted, that no rival claim exists, or that a fee exemption applies.

## After the award

Activate the namespace's Registrar, configure its native Resolver and begin [issuing names](/platform/issuing-names). A claim does not impose a mandatory annual Soran ownership fee. Optional hosted services and a namespace owner's finite-term policy are different arrangements.
