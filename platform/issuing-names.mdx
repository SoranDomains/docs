---
title: "Issuing names"
description: "Activate a Registrar, issue under its policy and maintain state availability."
---

After a namespace is awarded, activate its Registrar and issue names from the console or [Owner SDK](/owner/issuing-from-code). The namespace claim fee is separate from issuance.

## Activate the namespace

The owner chooses the Registrar's policy and authorizes deployment. Reclaimable policy lets the owner reclaim names under its rules. A zero-term non-reclaimable policy has no ownership expiry, but remains **unlocked** until the required [permanence transition](/concepts/ownership-guarantees) freezes eligible code and routing.

The console validates the selected deployment and transaction before signing. An uncertain submitted activation must be checked before retrying. Console team access alone cannot replace the on-chain owner's signature.

Deploy/configure a native Resolver for current memo-aware reads. A Registrar record alone is not an invitation to bypass Universal Lookup's strict payment checks.

## Issue names

Supply a canonical label and valid holder address. Issuance writes the holder and initial built-in target under the namespace policy. The holder can later publish its own complete Resolver payment instruction.

The console supports single issuance and CSV workflows with per-row outcomes. The Owner SDK supports batches of at most 23 names per transaction. Check confirmed outcomes and uncertain transaction hashes before retrying a row.

## Fees

The implemented naming contracts do not add a Soran per-name claim fee or a mandatory annual ownership charge. Stellar transaction/resource fees and storage rent still apply. Their amounts depend on current network parameters and transaction simulation; old displayed benchmark figures are not a current fee quote.

When enabled on a deployment, managed billing can charge for hosted services. A namespace owner can also offer its own commercial service or timed names under its disclosed contract policy. These costs are distinct from the upfront top-level namespace claim fee.

## Dormancy, TTL and restoration

A hosted **cold** label is a dormancy signal. It is not proof that Stellar has archived an entry, and it does not itself end ownership. A keeper may extend relevant contract, name, Resolver, reverse and Primary entries while they are available.

If a required entry is archived, current payment resolution may need restoration before it can succeed. The hosted API does not return a database payment destination in place of a failed on-chain read. A cached profile can be shown as historical information; it cannot establish current payment instructions.

## Who can change what

Namespace-owner authorization controls issuance, policy-permitted reclaim and renewal. Holder authorization controls a name's records and permitted individual transfer. Destination address authorization controls reverse/Primary identity for that address. An admin or issuer role in the console does not combine these authorities.
